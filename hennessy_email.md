Subject: Extending SEDA to AI-generated teacher scaffolds — an unusual application, and a reliability puzzle I'd value your view on

Dear Professor Hennessy,

I'm a teacher-educator at the Institute of Teacher Education (Gaya Campus, Sabah, Malaysia), and I've been building an LLM-based system for SPM English candidates. I'm writing because a central strand of the work applies SEDA in a direction I don't think it has been taken before, and your perspective would be genuinely valuable.

Briefly, the system is deliberately *not* an autonomous Socratic tutor. For our exam-conditioned, low-metacognition cohort I argue that relentless machine questioning risks reinforcing learned helplessness, so the system instead detects repeated failure and generates a teacher-facing intervention script that hands the student to a human — escalating the person, not the dialogue.

That raised a question I couldn't answer with a conventional classroom study: how do you evaluate the dialogic quality of a system that never talks to the student? My approach was to treat SEDA as an artifact audit — coding the machine-authored scripts against your eight clusters to see whether dialogic richness can be engineered into a scaffold even when the deployment itself is non-dialogic.

Two things from the pilot coding are why I'd love your view:

1. The scripts concentrate heavily in Invite elaboration/reasoning (IRE) and Guide direction (GD) — encouraging on the "invite reasoning" side, but more directive than I'd hoped.

2. Inter-rater reliability came in at only kappa ~ 0.40. Looking at the confusion, the coders struggled most to separate IRE from GD, and to place imperative/activity acts ("cut the draft into paragraphs", "draw a story mountain") that don't map cleanly onto a scheme built for live utterances. I've started to read this not as coder error but as a genuine boundary condition — SEDA applied to directive, non-conversational instructional text — and I suspect you'll have a much sharper reading of it than I do.

The manuscript is a Technical Action Research study (reliability engineering + this SEDA audit + a pending single-expert appraisal). I'm being careful not to overclaim: it's a design evaluation, not an efficacy study, and the expert appraisal is still to come.

Would you be open to a short conversation? Even 20–30 minutes on whether the artifact-audit use is defensible, and how you'd interpret that low kappa on directive text, would meaningfully shape the paper. I'm very happy to send the draft ahead if useful.

With thanks and respect for the scheme's influence on this work,
